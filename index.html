From silicon to strategy

Most salespeople learn the pitch. I learned the product first. I trained as an electronics and communication engineer, spent years in presales reading million-dollar tenders line by line, then carried a quota long enough to see where the hours actually go. Most of them go to work a script could do.

So I started writing the scripts. Scrapers that find companies showing a buying signal before anyone on the team has heard of them. Email infrastructure that gets read instead of filtered. Enrichment and routing that lands a qualified decision-maker in a sequence without a human copying a row. That foundation is also why clients trust the recommendation and not just the demo. Revenue is a system, and I'd rather build it than run it by hand.

I wrote up how I think about the shift in From Go-to-Market to Go-to-Market Engineering. The systems below are what it looks like in practice. On the side I ship products too: an HR document assistant on a self-hosted model, two community platforms, and a pair of mobile games, all live.

Systems I've built

Five pieces of go-to-market machinery, each one still running. The pattern is the same every time: find a signal, qualify it automatically, hand a human only the part that needs judgment.

Signal-based prospecting engine

FLYONIT · managed services

The team was working static lists top to bottom, which meant pitching a website refresh to companies that had just launched one.

A Python scanner sweeps Apollo regions for companies inside a tight ICP fence (10 to 50 employees, no government or nonprofits), checks each site for an outdated stack or AWS usage signals, and routes the hits back into Apollo to find decision-makers and enroll them in a signal-specific sequence. Verticals are rebalanced by hit history: accounting, dental, and solar get most of the search budget because that's where qualified hits actually come from. Runs twice a week as a scheduled job on an always-on Mac mini; earlier versions ran on GitHub Actions and a cloud VM.

Email-security signal track

FLYONIT · cybersecurity

Cybersecurity outreach to businesses that already had it handled was wasted motion. The ones with the real gap were invisible from a list.

The scanner checks a prospect's actual sending domain for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC enforcement, and only enrolls companies whose mail could be spoofed today. A nightly nationwide sweep rotates through states and verticals, caps enrollment per run, and feeds a three-step sequence written around the exact gap it found. The email lands with evidence instead of a generic pitch.

Outbound email infrastructure

FLYONIT · all campaigns

Cold email was landing in spam and the metrics made it look like a messaging problem. It was a plumbing problem.

Set up sending across four domains with mailbox rotation managed at the sequence level, proper authentication on every domain, and volume caps that keep reputation intact. Read rates climbed above 80% and reply rates tripled on the same copy. The lesson I keep repeating to people: fix deliverability before touching the subject line.

AI receptionist prospecting

FLYONIT · AI receptionist offer

The pitch for an AI receptionist only lands with businesses already losing calls. No list filter tells you who those are. Their customers do.

A crawler reads public reviews on Yelp and similar sites for local service businesses and flags the ones where customers complain about unanswered phones, long hold times, or no callback. Each hit gets enriched to a decision-maker and enrolled in a short sequence that quotes the complaint back to them, with the AI receptionist as the fix. The prospect reads their own one-star review before they read the pitch.

Public RFP radar

Personal project · live on this site

Government and nonprofit RFPs are public records, but they sit scattered across dozens of portals. The services that aggregate them charge $30 to $160 a month, and most of the portals block scrapers outright.

A Python pipeline that only reads sources published for reuse: the SAM.gov bulk extract, the Grants.gov API, CanadaBuys open data, and city open-data portals. It dedupes amendments and cross-source repeats, has Claude write a one-line summary and category for each notice, and filters down to work that fits digital, marketing, data, and consulting firms. Runs every morning on GitHub Actions, publishes a live board here, and goes out as a free weekly newsletter on Tuesdays. Nothing in it comes from a portal that could ban it.
